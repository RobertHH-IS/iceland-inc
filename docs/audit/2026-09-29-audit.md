Seven auditors reviewed the kernel, the Iceland model, the interface and the harness. Two or three independent reviewers then tried to refute each finding.
Of 70 findings, 62 were upheld and 8 refuted. Fixes are tracked in the implementation pass that follows.

## High (8)

### H1. No check catches impossible balance sheets: holders' assets, issuers' liabilities and real capital go negative while all four checks pass
- Area: kernel-accounting · `src/core/checks.ts:32` · logic · upheld 3/3
- measureChecks checks only row sums, instrument sums, ΔNW against saving plus other changes, and position changes against their postings. Nothing checks the sign a position must have given its role: a holder's asset should be ≥ 0, an issuer's liability ≤ 0, and a real asset ≥ 0. In the Iceland model, ordinary lever settings inside the allowed ranges produce overdrafts on deposits, short positions in government bonds, firms whose 'loan' becomes an asset, a negative stock of government bonds outstanding, and negative physical capital. Every accounting residual stays at or below 5e-12. The harness property test (src/harness/layers.ts:281) only requires finite values and passing checks, so it reports these runs as PASS.
- Suggested fix: 1. Add a fifth diagnostic, 'position-sign', to measureChecks. For every j with role[j] = holder it requires pos[j] ≥ −tol; for role[j] = issuer, pos[j] ≤ tol; for real instruments, pos ≥ −tol. It needs role in CheckSpec.
2. Report the first violation (instrument, player, month) in CheckReport. Make the harness property and robustness layers fail on it; it can be a warning in the UI.
3. Then fix the model rules that allow it: floors on PF and W bond trades and on firms' net repayment (borrowing ≥ −loans/dt), a non-negative outstanding government debt, and non-negative gross investment.
- Reviewer's refinement: 1. Keep `measureChecks`/`CHECKS` as the four accounting identities. Add a separate diagnostic, for example `measureSigns(L, spec)` in checks.ts. `spec` gets `role` and `financial` from the compiled model. The rule: a holder's position must be at least −tolSign, an issuer's position at most +tolSign, and a real asset at least −tolSign. Give it its own tolerance, for example 1e-6, not the 1e-9 accounting tolerance.

2. Let an instrument opt out with a new field, for example `InstrumentDef.signed?: 'either'`, so the generic kernel can host net-position instruments. Record this as a kernel contract change in a new decision doc.

3. Record the first violation for each position (instrument, player, month, value). Report them in a new `CheckReport.signViolations` field, not in `failures` or `maxResiduals`. Do not throw on them. Show them as a warning in the UI.

4. In the same change as step 5, make harness layer 6 (property and robustness tests) and the golden scenarios fail on any violation.

5. Fix the model rules. Write each floor as a regime of the existing rule (`combine` and `regime`), not as a second equation.
   - Firms' borrowing is at least −businessLoans/dt.
   - Gross investment is at least 0, so capital only decays.
   - Bond sales by PF, B, HO and W are at most their holdings/dt.
   - Purchases paid from deposits (PF foreign assets and bonds, W carry trades, households' bond buying) are capped by the payer's deposits, or financed by selling another asset.
   - The government's buyback (negative new issuance) is capped by what holders can sell. Any surplus beyond that stays in `treasuryAccount`, which keeps govBonds/G at or below 0.
   - Give households' deposits a floor too, for example borrowing or consumption adjusting when deposits run out. `incomeTax` +5 drives deposits/HW negative.

6. Update decision 0002 §6 "No floors in extreme settings" to describe what is now floored. If any calibration results move, report the change.
- Reviewer's refinement: 1. **Diagnostic.** Keep `measureChecks` and `CHECKS` as the four accounting identities.
   - Add a separate position-sign diagnostic to the engine, built from `role` (from `compile`). It measures the breach each step: holder pos < −tol; issuer pos > tol, which covers real assets too since they have holders only.
   - Record the first breach (instrument, player, month, value), much as `failures` are recorded now.
   - Allow an optional per-instrument opt-out, e.g. an `InstrumentDef` flag for net-position instruments. Nothing in either model needs it today; the reference model shows no violations.
   - Record this as a kernel contract change in a new docs/decisions record, and update architecture §4.4 and §6.

2. **Harness.** Make the property layer fail on a sign breach.
   - Add a robustness sweep that runs every lever at its min and at its max for 240 months. The 40 random runs of 1 to 4 events can miss cases; the sweep currently finds 38 lever-setting/instrument pairs with violations.
   - In the UI, show a sign breach as a warning.

3. **Model rules.** The floors must cover every channel, not just the four listed. Each floor should be a `combine` with a named `regime` (AGENTS.md rule 4). Floors do not bind at baseline, but rerun the harness and update the golden scenarios on purpose.
   - **Government buybacks** (`bondIssue` < 0): cap them at banks' holdings, not just at total outstanding debt. With a key rate of 0, banks' holdings go negative at month 192 while 12% of GDP is still outstanding. Any surplus beyond the cap stays in the treasury account.
   - **Bond trades with banks as counterparty** (PF, HO, W buying or selling): a seller cannot sell more than it holds, and buyers cannot take more than banks hold.
   - **Pension funds:** cap purchases of bonds and foreign assets at their deposits, which keeps deposits/PF ≥ 0.
   - **Non-residents (W):** their deposits are drained mainly by balance-of-payments flows (pfForeign −20 at month 3, kronaShock −25 at month 8), not by bond trades. So add a liquidity term to `bondPurchasesW`: W sells bonds when its deposits fall toward a floor. Alternatively, close the gap through the exchange rate.
   - **Firms:** borrowing ≥ −loans/dt; cash left over stays as deposits.
   - **Firm investment:** gross investment ≥ 0, which keeps capital and depreciation ≥ 0.
   - **Households:** a liquidity constraint on spending, or explicit unsecured credit, so deposits/HW and deposits/HY stay ≥ 0. Tax +10 and VAT +10 currently overdraw them.
- Reviewer's refinement: Leave measureChecks and CHECKS as the four accounting identities. Put the sign test in a separate plausibility diagnostic, for example measureSigns(L, role, financial, tol) → {worst, first: {instrument, player, month}}. That way the fixed length-4 residual array and the 1e-9 accounting semantics are not changed. Report it from the engine. In the harness property and robustness layers, fail on it for instrument/player pairs that are not explicitly exempt. In the UI, show it as a warning.

Let a PlayerDef or InstrumentDef declare `mayGoNegative` for buffers the model knowingly nets, such as deposits/W as a króna overdraft. Each such flag needs a provenance note, and the exemptions need listing in a decision doc. Without that, the fix forces floors on every buffer stock.

Then fix the rules. Write each floor as a `combine`/`regime` rule, as AGENTS.md rule 4 requires:
(a) Gross investment ≥ 0 for every firm: investment = max(0, target), regime 'No new investment'. With δ·dt < 1 this keeps capital ≥ 0.
(b) Firms' borrowing ≥ −businessLoans/dt. The excess then accumulates as deposits.
(c) bondPurchasesPF ≥ −govBonds/PF/dt. The remaining liquidity need must go somewhere explicit: sell indexed or bank bonds, or slow the foreign-asset purchases. A floor alone would push PF deposits further negative.
(d) Allocate government buybacks in proportion to holdings, not by issue shares. Cap them at the outstanding stock, and let the treasury account absorb further surpluses.
(e) For deposits/W, either exempt it as a documented overdraft or give non-residents an alternative buffer, such as selling govBonds/W. The auditor's 'floor on W bond trades' does not address it, because W's violation is in deposits, not bonds.

Floors change results. Rerun bun test, typecheck and the harness. Report any calibration moves, update the golden scenarios deliberately, and revise the 'No floors' known gap in docs/decisions/0002-iceland-port.md §6.

### H2. A negative 'purchase' makes the seller pay for capital the buyer scraps, and drives real capital negative
- Area: kernel-accounting · `src/core/ledger.ts:89` · economics · upheld 2/3
- postLeg's purchase branch is linear in a. With a < 0, settle() makes the seller pay the buyer. L.move shrinks the buyer's real asset, and L.income[to] books negative income for the seller. The asset is not returned to the seller (FC is itself a holder of 'capital'); it simply disappears. The seller's net worth falls by |a| for scrapping someone else's capital, while the buyer's is unchanged. That is not a reversal of a sale, and negative gross fixed capital formation has no economic meaning. Nothing rejects or flags a negative purchase amount; types.ts:226 even says a negative amount 'reverses the posting'. In the Iceland model, firms.ts investmentRule (around lines 87-106) has an unbounded profits term. Under a lever setting inside its range, the smelters' investment turns negative and stays negative. Their capital then falls through zero, and depreciation (rate × capital) turns negative, which adds to their net worth every month.
- Suggested fix: In the kernel, treat purchase amounts < 0 as an error: record a check failure such as 'negative-purchase' (or throw in dev), and document in LegDef that purchase amounts must be ≥ 0. A sale of used capital back to another holder should be modelled as a 'trade'. In the Iceland model, floor gross investment at zero in firms.ts, e.g. investmentPurchase = max(0, …) or a smooth floor. Capital then shrinks only through depreciation.
- Reviewer's refinement: 1. Leave the kernel's handling of negative purchases alone. A negative leg amount is a documented reversal (types.ts:226, decision 0001 row 3), the accounting stays consistent, and netting purchases is legitimate.

2. Fix the Iceland model instead: floor each sector's investment target at zero in firms.ts investmentRule. Following AGENTS.md rule 4 and the Taylor-rule example in docs/authoring.md, add:
```ts
combine: (t) => Math.max(0, t.normal + t.profits + t.realRate + (t.capacity ?? 0)),
regime: (_c, _v, t) => (t.normal + t.profits + t.realRate + (t.capacity ?? 0) < 0 ? 'Investment stopped' : null),
```
Also update explain.rule to say "…, never below zero". This works for three reasons:
- `combine` produces the target before `adjust` is applied (types.ts:151-155).
- The exponential adjustment moves investment a fraction k = 1 − exp(−lamInv·dt), between 0 and 1, of the way to the target each month, so investment stays ≥ 0.
- Capital then follows K' = K(1 − δ·dt) + I·dt with δ·dt = 0.08/12, so it stays ≥ 0, and so does depreciation.

The floor never binds at baseline, where the target is i0 > 0, so drift and calibration should not change. Run the harness to confirm.

3. Optionally, add a kernel diagnostic or a harness robustness check that flags any holder whose real-asset position (such as 'capital') goes below zero. That targets the actual invariant, rather than rejecting negative purchase amounts.

4. Note separately that at the most negative aluminium prices the smelters make losses of about 2 % of GDP a year for 20 years, and their foreign parents inject cash through negative dividends. The model has no way for the smelters to shut down or cut output. That is a separate known limitation worth documenting in decision 0002 §6.
- Reviewer's refinement: If the owners want to fix it, do not touch the kernel. Signed, linear postings are the documented contract (`types.ts` LegDef; decision 0001, change #3), and making negative purchases an error would break it. Put the fix in the Iceland model instead.

1. In `firms.ts` `investmentRule`, keep gross real investment non-negative. Use a smooth floor on the adjusted target, for example softplus with a small scale, or `max(0, …)` with a regime label such as "No new investment". A smooth floor keeps the income–spending block and the Newton step well behaved.
2. With that floor, capital can shrink only through depreciation, at the rate × capital, which stays at or above zero.
3. Optionally, add a harness property that real capital holdings stay at or above zero across the random lever runs.
4. If nothing is changed, name "negative investment and capital for loss-making exporters at extreme lever settings" as an example in decision 0002 §6, "No floors in extreme settings".
- Reviewer's refinement: In `src/models/iceland/modules/firms.ts`, change `investmentRule(j)` so the target is floored at zero. Add:
- `combine: (t) => Math.max(0, Object.values(t).reduce((a, b) => a + b, 0))`
- `regime: (_c, _v, t) => (Σ t < 0 ? 'No new investment: capital only wears out' : null)`

AGENTS.md rule 4 requires a combine or regime for non-additive rules. Also extend `explain.rule` to say investment never falls below zero.

Floor `investment<j>` itself, not `investmentPurchase<j>`, so that `investmentReal`, FC's profit, imports and GDP stay consistent with the cash purchase flow. A smooth floor is also fine. With the exponential partial adjustment toward a target of zero or more, capital then only shrinks by depreciation. In the scratch test at aluminium −40, capital/XA reaches 0.49 at month 240 instead of −1.01, and the accounting checks still pass.

For the kernel and harness, do not make a negative purchase an error or a check failure; the posting is a correct, documented linear reversal. At most:
- add a non-fatal compile or run diagnostic, and
- add a harness robustness check that no holder's real-asset position (`capital`, homes) goes below zero in the lever-range and random-lever runs.

Also:
- clarify in the LegDef docs that a negative purchase un-produces the asset (it is not a resale), and that a used-asset sale should be modelled as a `trade`;
- add this case to decision 0002 §6 if the floor is not adopted.

### H3. unitScale assumes every rate/ratio/expectation is a fraction, so variables held in %, pp or % of GDP are weighted 100x in ideasAtPlay
- Area: kernel-runtime · `src/core/format.ts:59` · units · upheld 3/3
- unitScale returns 0.01 (1 pp as a fraction) for every variable of kind 'rate', 'ratio' or 'expectation' and never looks at the unit. Three shipped variables of those kinds are held in percent units: keyRateSuggestion ('%/yr'), taxRuleSuggestion ('pp'), and the reference model's debtRatio ('% of GDP', value 55). ideasAtPlay divides their changes by 0.01, so a 1 pp move counts as 100 'units'. In the default Manual mode this makes the Taylor rule the top idea at play, even though the rule does nothing there.
- Suggested fix: Use the unit in unitScale, for example `case 'rate': case 'ratio': case 'expectation': return /GDP/i.test(v.unit) || /^\s*(%|pp)/.test(v.unit) ? 1 : 0.01;`. Also add a compile warning for rate/ratio variables whose unit is neither a fraction, 'ratio', 'log points' nor a percent form, so a new unit cannot silently get the wrong scale.
- Reviewer's refinement: The proposed fix is correct as written. Optionally, match whole unit tokens, e.g. `case 'rate': case 'ratio': case 'expectation': return /GDP/i.test(v.unit) || /^\s*(%|pp\b)/.test(v.unit) ? 1 : 0.01;`. Add the proposed compile warning for rate/ratio/expectation units outside {fraction, fraction/yr, ratio, log points, %…, pp…, …GDP…}. Add unitScale tests for '%/yr', 'pp' and '% of GDP' in tests/core/scenario.test.ts.

### H4. Cash consumption is deflated by a CPI whose 24.5% housing part follows house prices, so house-price moves create fake changes in real output
- Area: model-households-prices · `src/models/iceland/modules/households.ts:188` · economics · upheld 3/3
- realConsumption = consumption / cpi (households.ts:188), and output starts from realConsumption (firms.ts:407-409). The CPI gives 24.5% weight to housingCost (prices.ts:93), which follows house prices (housing.ts:82-90). In the calibration, 20.9 of that 24.5 is imputed rent. The nominal consumption flow already contains owner-occupiers' housing services as a cash payment to FR at baseline: firms.ts:169 says FR profit includes 'the housing services homeowners provide themselves'. But that flow is set by the consumption function and does not rise or fall with housingCost. When house prices move, the CPI moves, and the model reads this as a change in the real quantity consumed, even though the housing stock is fixed and no cash moves. Output, retail and services value added, jobs and the output gap then follow that false signal. The same division by CPI is used for builders' real repair sales (firms.ts:429).
- Suggested fix: Deflate the nominal consumption flow by a price index that matches it. Option (a): use a consumption deflator without housing, pC = vatFactor × (omD·Pd + omM·Pm)/(omD+omM), for realConsumption and for the /cpi in firms.ts real sales (repairs, FR sales). Keep the full CPI for indexation, inflation, expectations and the policy rule. Option (b): split consumption into housing services = (omH share of baseline C) × housingCost × fixed quantity, paid to FR as imputed rent, plus the rest from the consumption function, so the nominal side moves with the housing price. Then re-tune betaC and betaRI (both were tuned to the rate-shock output trough) and update the golden scenarios.
- Reviewer's refinement: Use option (a), with one refinement. Deflate the nominal consumption flow by a consumption price index that stays neutral under general inflation and ignores changes in imputed rent. For example, pCons = pGoods × (1 − omRent) + omRent × housingCost, where pGoods = vatFactor × (omD·Pd + omM·Pm)/(omD + omM) and omRent is the 3.6 actual-rent weight. Actual rents are cash payments. The imputed part (20.9) follows goods prices. Dropping all housing, as option (a) does, is an acceptable simpler version.

Do not hold the imputed part at a fixed price of 1, as the auditor's test script does: under trend inflation (start-from-today) that creates growth in real consumption that isn't real.

Use pCons in:
- realConsumption (households.ts:188)
- salesFC's repairs term (firms.ts:429)
- any other place where a household-spending cash flow is turned into a volume (the import volumes then follow automatically through realConsumption)

Keep the full CPI for indexation, inflation, expectations, wage bargaining, real incomes and the policy rule. Add the imputed and actual rent weights as data parameters (VIS01306, Dec-2025). Then re-tune betaC and betaRI and update the golden scenarios on purpose.

Avoid option (b) as written. Paying the extra imputed rent to FR in cash would inflate FR profit, which is taxed at tauF, drives investment through betaPi, and is paid out as dividends under fixed shares to other age groups. That would create false tax, investment and redistribution effects. If imputed rent is ever repriced in nominal terms, book it as a non-cash imputation that is both income and spending of the owner-occupier group itself.
- Reviewer's refinement: The direction of the fix is right. Option (a) as written would leave a mirror-image distortion, so apply it consistently.

1. Define pC = vatFactor × (omD·Pd + omM·Pm)/(omD+omM). Use it for `realConsumption` (households.ts:188) and the builders' repairs term (firms.ts:429). `importsConsumer`, `importsInputsFR` and `importsInputsFC` read `realConsumption` and follow automatically.
2. In the consumption function (households.ts), also move the CPI-scaled terms onto pC:
   - the autonomous term c0{g} × cpi;
   - the wealth anchor cpi × LW0{g};
   - the housing-wealth term × cpi.
   Otherwise about 29% of baseline consumption (the c0 terms, 14.87 of 51.6) moves in nominal terms with house prices while its deflator does not. In my in-memory test this changed the rate-shock output at m36 from −0.638 to −0.604.
3. Keep the full CPI for:
   - indexation and inflation, expectations and the policy rule;
   - `realDisposableIncome` (the house-price driver);
   - profit smoothing.
4. With option (a), the rate-output-trough measure moves from about −0.48 to about −0.60 to −0.64, the edge of its range [−0.6, −0.25] or past it. So:
   - re-tune betaC and betaRI and check rate-output-timing and fiscal-output-year1;
   - update the golden scenarios;
   - record the change in a decision doc, since the v1 E38 equation changes.
5. For the start-from-today work, month-0 `realConsumption` must be re-anchored with pC, because at the start pC will not equal the CPI.

Option (b) is the cleaner long-run design: explicit imputed-rent services at omH × housingCost × a fixed quantity, paid to FR and returned as owners' income. It needs the same re-tuning.
- Reviewer's refinement: Use option (a) for `realConsumption`: deflate by pC = vatFactor × (omD·domesticPrice + omM·importPrice)/(omD+omM). Consumption includes VAT, so a price index that includes VAT is the right one here.

For builders' real repair sales (firms.ts:429), deflate by a price before VAT: (omD·Pd + omM·Pm)/(omD+omM) without vatFactor, or simply `domesticPrice`, since builders are domestic producers.

The reason: that term's numerator is maintShare × (consumption − vat), which equals maintShare × C/(1+vatRate), so it is already net of VAT. Dividing it by a price index that includes vatFactor removes VAT a second time whenever the VAT rate moves. The current division by the CPI has this flaw too, and plain option (a) would keep it.

Keep the full CPI, with its housing part, for indexation, inflation, expectations, wages and the stabiliser rule. Then:
1. Re-check `betaC` and `betaRI` against the rate-shock checks. With the fix the output trough is about −0.64% against the [−0.6, −0.25] range.
2. Deliberately update the golden scenarios and the calibration results in the pull request.

Option (b), an explicit imputed-rent flow at housingCost × fixed quantity, is only consistent if FR's extra imputed-rent profit also returns to households as owner income in the same month. Otherwise it drains households' cash budgets. Option (a) is the simpler, safer change.

### H5. PPP term sits in the short-run króna target, so a world-price shock is mostly cancelled by the króna within a quarter
- Area: model-firms-external-money · `src/models/iceland/modules/external.ts:199` · economics · upheld 2/3
- The module header and v1 SPEC describe PPP as a long-run anchor. In the code, however, the PPP term log(Pd_lastMonth / worldPrice) is part of the target that logExchangeRate chases at lamFX = 12/yr (line 197), which closes about 63% of the gap each month. The term reads the current worldPrice, so when the 'World prices' lever (importPrices) moves, the target moves at once and the króna appreciates by nearly the full amount within about two months. As a result the lever acts mainly as a nominal appreciation. Import prices and the CPI hardly move for a year, and the fish and aluminium revenue windfall promised in the lever's definition (line 573: 'fish and aluminium revenue jumps at once') is mostly undone by month 2. No calibration check uses importPrices, so the harness does not catch this.
- Suggested fix: Take PPP out of the fast target. One option is a slow state variable pppAnchor that moves toward log(Pd/P*) at a long-run speed (say 0.2–0.3 per year, a half-life of a few years), used in the logExchangeRate target in place of the instantaneous ratio. Another is to let lamFX apply only to the sentiment, carry and portfolio terms. Then add a calibration check for the world-prices lever (e.g. CPI pass-through after 4–8 quarters) and re-run the 'Króna −10%: price level' check, which also depends on this term.
- Reviewer's refinement: The auditor's main idea is right. Take the PPP term out of the fast lamFX target and give it a slow state of its own:
- Add a new state `pppAnchor`, with initial value 0.
- Its rule is BEHAVIOUR, with `inputs: ['worldPrice']` and `lagInputs: ['domesticPrice']`. It moves toward `log(lastMonth(domesticPrice) / worldPrice)` with `adjust: { speed: 'lamPPP', form: 'exponential' }`.
- The `ppp` term of logExchangeRate then reads `c.v('pppAnchor')`.

Do not simply set lamPPP to 0.2–0.3 a year. Tested, that fails two existing checks:
- wage-price-level-6y: 2.87 against [3, 8]
- krona-price-level-8q: 1.48 against [1.5, 3]

At 0.5, wage-price-level-6y still fails (3.00). A speed of about 1 a year passes all 28 checks and still keeps most of the world-price windfall: at month 3 the króna has moved −3.5% instead of −15%, fish revenue is +16.6% instead of +2.4%, and CPI is +4.4% at month 12 instead of +1.9%.

Either:
- (a) use lamPPP ≈ 1 a year, provenance `calibrated`, and record in a decision doc that it is faster than the 3–5-year half-life in the literature because the price-level checks need it; or
- (b) use the literature speed and deliberately retune whatever lifts the price-level responses (pass-through or wage–price parameters), then re-verify both checks.

In either case:

- Add a calibration check for the world-prices lever. For example: CPI pass-through at 4–8 quarters is a sizeable share of the import-basket weight times the shock, and the króna appreciation at month 3 is well below the full offset.

### H6. Gross investment has no floor: smelter investment and even the smelters' physical capital go negative
- Area: model-firms-external-money · `src/models/iceland/modules/firms.ts:100` · economics · upheld 3/3
- The investment target is i0 × [1 + betaPi × (smoothed real after-tax profit ÷ baseline − 1) − …] with no floor. The smelters' baseline profit is thin (piXA0 ≈ 0.33% of GDP) and their alumina bill does not follow the aluminium price. An in-range aluminium price cut therefore gives profit/π0 of about −5, a negative investment target, and negative investment purchases: a negative XA→FC leg on the 'investment' flow, as if builders were buying machines back. Held long enough, the smelters' capital stock (a real asset) becomes negative, and depreciation at 8% of a negative stock becomes a gain.
- Suggested fix: Floor gross investment at zero in investmentRule, e.g. a combine of Math.max(0, Σ terms) with a regime label such as 'No new investment'. Also bound the profit term so thin-margin sectors do not swing without limit, for instance by scaling it by capital or value added instead of baseline profit, or by capping profit/π0 − 1 at about −1.
